04 / FIELD KNOWLEDGE

Crop & Plant Health Guide

Practical starting points for common crops grown in Iraq and the Kurdistan Region. Local weather, variety, soil, and season change the advice.

EDUCATIONAL GUIDE
POTATO

Water & care

Keep soil evenly moist while tubers develop, but avoid waterlogging. Water near the soil rather than repeatedly wetting leaves. Uneven moisture can affect tuber quality.

Watch for late blight

Dark, water-soaked-looking leaf patches that spread quickly in cool, humid, wet conditions can be a warning. Isolate suspect plants and ask a local agricultural extension specialist before choosing treatment.

TOMATO

Water & care

Water the root zone deeply enough to reach roots, then let the soil condition guide the next watering. Avoid frequent shallow watering and sudden large swings in moisture. Use support and spacing for airflow.

Watch for early blight

Older leaves may develop brown spots with concentric rings and yellowing around them. Remove badly affected fallen leaves, avoid wetting foliage, and confirm the diagnosis locally.

CUCUMBER

Water & care

Cucumbers need regular moisture, especially during flowering and fruit growth. Hot, windy weather and sandy soil may mean more frequent checks. Water at the base and provide good airflow.

Watch for powdery mildew

White, powder-like patches on leaves can indicate powdery mildew. Avoid overcrowding, remove heavily affected plant material safely, and seek local advice if it spreads.

PEPPER & EGGPLANT

Water & care

Check soil moisture around the root zone and water consistently during flowering and fruiting. Avoid leaving roots in standing water. Mulch can reduce evaporation where suitable.

Watch for aphids

Small insects clustered on tender shoots or leaf undersides, curling leaves, or sticky honeydew may indicate aphids. Inspect plants regularly and ask a local specialist about crop-safe control.

WHEAT & BARLEY

Seasonal crops

These are important cereal crops in Iraq. Water needs depend strongly on rainfall, growth stage, soil, and local irrigation access. Use locally recommended planting dates and irrigation schedules rather than a universal timetable.

Watch for rust diseases

Orange, brown, or yellow powdery pustules on leaves or stems can be a warning sign. Take a clear photo and consult an agricultural extension service for confirmation.

ALL CROPS

Before watering

Check soil a few centimetres below the surface near the roots, consider the crop's growth stage, and check recent rain and heat. A wilted plant does not always mean it needs water; root problems and disease can also cause wilting.

Important

These are general education notes, not a diagnosis or a precise local irrigation prescription. Avoid applying pesticides or mixing chemicals based only on an online description.

06 / PROJECT INFORMATION

A credible path from concept to evidence

The strongest project is clear about what it knows — and what it still needs to prove.

STAGE 01

Concept & research

Define the water problem, compare moisture-capture approaches, and identify measurable goals.

FOUNDATION
STAGE 02

Small demonstration

Build a safe mesh-and-mist demonstration, measure collected droplets, record conditions, and repeat trials.

PLANNED
STAGE 03

Test & improve

Compare results, add appropriate sensors, evaluate energy use, and investigate water quality with suitable testing.

FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

What must be measured?

  • Air temperature and relative humidity during each trial.
  • Volume collected over a defined time, including a control trial.
  • Mesh material, area, airflow, and mist-source conditions.
  • Energy consumed by any powered components.
  • Repeatability and possible contamination risks.

Responsible project claims

  • Do not describe humidifier mist capture as extraction from dry air.
  • Do not claim water is safe to drink without appropriate testing.
  • Do not report yields, efficiency, or savings without measured results.
  • Keep electronics away from water; use safe low-voltage components with adult supervision.
